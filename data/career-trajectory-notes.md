## The Dilemma

A separate mentor (outside career-ops) flagged that Andrea's trajectory reads as closer to "economic simulation scientist" — private sector/government — than a classic academic or central-bank economist track.

Andrea's framing: good at modelling, genuinely enjoys it, but doesn't want to spend the rest of his career writing code and running simulations — wants to move toward a more conceptual level.

**Key reframe:** the fix is altitude, not sector. The conceptual work Andrea wants already shows up at the top of his own CV (GLRI — deciding what to measure and how to say it to ministers, not running the regressions). The lever is title/seniority band: Principal Economist, Head of Research, Director of Economic Research, Chief Economist — roles where the job structurally shifts from building the model to setting the research agenda and directing others who build it. This is an extension of what he already did at Whiteshield, not a pivot away from his specialization.

**Caution flagged:** "Macro Strategist" titles at banks/asset managers (see report `149-state-street-macro-strategy-analyst-avp-2026-06-22.md`) often sound conceptual but are frequently hands-on, market-data-driven note-writing — check actual day-to-day scope in the JD, not just the title.

## The 40s Limbo

Andrea: too senior for junior roles, not enough *recognized tenure* for Principal/Head/Chief titles at large institutions. This is a structural mid-career specialist trap, not a capability gap — large institutions (central banks, IFIs, big banks) gate senior titles by tenure inside their own ladder, and Andrea's real scope never compounded inside any single ladder.

### Four ways out (discussed, none committed to yet)

1. **Go where the ladder is shorter** — smaller/leaner orgs (boutiques, smaller think tanks, regional development banks, newer units). Whiteshield is Andrea's own proof case: Senior Data Scientist → Senior Economist in two years there.
2. **Go where the ladder doesn't exist yet** — CBDC units, climate-risk modelling teams at regulators, AI-in-economics groups: fields under ~10 years old, where nobody has 15 years of experience yet. Probably the best structural fit given the ABM/heterogeneous-agent niche.
3. **Build the title before an org gives it** — a fractional/independent advisor stint, self-declared scope plus a visible client list, used later to parachute into a permanent Head-of role.
4. **Negotiate on scope, not years** — when a posting's banding doesn't fit, explicitly ask whether the org can title and pay for the scope Andrea would actually own, rather than accepting the posted band as fixed.

### Supporting tactics

- **Use the Oxford contract end date (September 2026) as a forcing function** — a natural trigger to commit to one of the four paths deliberately, rather than drifting into another 2-year project by default (the path of least resistance given the trajectory pattern above).
- **Build the public "conceptual" credential before any job requires it** — writing, op-eds, conference talks on macro-financial transmission. Andrea already does this in miniature (GLG council seat, continued academic publishing, the Davos launch) and could deliberately expand it. Judged on substance, not job-title history — a side door around the years-vs-titles gate.
- **Check whether the dream-job institution already has the flatter unit needed.** The Bank of England's published research agenda (see `[[dream_job_boe]]` memory) leans into heterogeneous-agent/agent-based modelling — that specific team may be young/flat enough to not gate by the same institutional tenure as the broader BoE economist ladder. Same logic applies to BIS Innovation Hub and ECB's newer modelling units.
